# Your first walk

Welcome to Alix. This is a walk: you read a deck one card at a time, think
about each question, reveal the answer, and say how it went. Nothing is
scheduled yet; a walk is how you meet a deck before you study it. The nine
cards below each teach one thing about Alix by having you do it, and the walk
picks up where you left off. This text is the deck's topic, the background
its cards share; you can bring it back on any card.

## Reveal this answer, then end the card with one of the three buttons under it. What do they mean?
Missed it, Partly, Got it: how recalling went, in your own honest judgement.
> [!NOTE]
> In a walk the result is remembered: a card you got stays out of the next
> walk, one you missed or partly knew comes back. In a review the same three
> grades schedule the card instead, sooner after a miss, later after a hit.

## This deck has a topic: the text that framed it before the first card. Open it again with the Context chip, then reveal.
The Context chip opens the card's topic while the question is still on screen.
> [!NOTE]
> A topic opens by itself the first time a walk reaches one of its cards.
> After that it waits behind the chip (or the `c` key on the web) until the

## Reveal this answer, hide it again, then bring it back. What is hiding for?
A second try at recalling before you grade, with the answer out of sight.
> [!NOTE]
> On the phone, tap the revealed answer to hide it and tap the sheet's × to
> bring it back. On the web, press `h` or click the answer; the corner
> marker says what the next press does. A choice pick, a typed check and a
> sketch have nothing to hide.

## What is there beyond a walk?
Reviews at a depth, the AI exam, the tutor, and workspaces.
> [!NOTE]
> A review schedules cards and asks at a depth: Recognize picks from
> choices, Recall answers from memory, Reconstruct rebuilds the answer in
> your own words. A typed card compares your text with the answer, whole or
> line by line; an explain card has you write first, then tick the key
> points you covered; a drawing card takes a sketch. The AI exam checks
> that you understood a deck built from a source, not just memorized it,
> and the tutor helps on any card. A workspace is a folder of decks
> reviewed together, sharing settings and a name, with its own progress.
